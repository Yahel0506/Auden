## Player

- **Songs change like a carousel.** When the song changes, the covers and titles run past instead of swapping.
- **A paused cover rests.** Pausing shrinks the cover and drains its colour, and an animated cover's clip pauses with it.
- **Hold the player bar.** Holding the player in the navigation bar opens the song's menu with its card on top — cover, title, artist and album — and a tap on the card opens the album.
- **A lossless source, Monochrome.** Official builds carry Monochrome as a source of lossless audio. It is off by default: switch it on under Sources. Downloads are no longer FLAC-only, and High quality leaves out the heavy hi-res files, keeping lossy and CD-quality FLAC.
- **Share lyrics as a video.** Picking lines to share opens a story you can save as a video: the song plays the picked stretch while the lines are lit as in the lyrics panel — the sweep, the lift of each word, held words swelling — over the player's backdrop or the song's animated cover, blurred. Switch to Video or Image, and show or hide the Auden mark.
- **Queue swipes.** Swipe a queue row left to remove it, or right to move it into Next in queue (or play it next, as your setting says); the row slides away and the list closes over it.
- **Undo in the queue.** Removing a song or clearing Next in queue offers Undo for a few seconds, and the queue comes back as it was.
- **Sharper covers.** YouTube covers are asked at the large size the screen needs and fall back to the one every video has when a video has no larger copy, so none goes blank.
- **Livelier lyrics.** The line being sung keeps its glow and feathered sweep on the main player's strip, on the extended lyrics screen while you scroll, and while you pick lines to share, with no hard edge around the glow.
- **Matching without accents.** A title or an artist now matches its spelling without accents — Şımarık and Simarik, Müslüm Gürses and Muslum Gurses — and a CJK title's romanized aside, accents and all, files the upload that carries only the romanization.

## Home

- **Auden on Home.** The name now sits beside the mark at the top of Home.
- **A Home that starts generic and becomes yours.** A new listener gets YouTube Music's own recommendations, videos and new releases — asked of the catalogue as nobody, with no account behind them. From the first songs you play, Home starts to change: shelves of your own — suggestions seeded by your favourite songs and artists — grow while the generic ones give way, and at about sixty songs heard it is all yours. What you played lately stays right under your Top Picks, every song in the order you played it.
- **Top Picks that are lists you can use.** Up to eight lists are made for you each day from what you play, each with a moving cover in the hand of the discovery station — filled shapes in one family of colours: circling discs for In loop, folding arrows for your likes, a turning burst, sliding bands in the genre's colours, a funnel, a sunset, a calendar mosaic — with its own songs' sleeves on it, a play button and a page with the same cover, tinted and carried on like every list's: **In loop** (the ones you can't stop playing), **New music for you**, **Your Likes Mix**, **Because you like…** a song you repeat, a **genre mix** of only artists you already play, **Deep cuts** from the artist you play most, **Rediscover** (old favourites you stopped playing) and **One year ago** (what you played this week last year). The genre mix finds your artists' genres on its own, with no setup.
- **New from your artists.** A row of the albums and singles your most played artists have put out in the last two years, newest first.

## Library

- **A bubble for your photo.** On the Library, your photo has a bubble of its own beside the one with the new playlist button and a ⋯.
- **Edit pinned and sections.** The ⋯ offers to edit the pinned lists — they shake, and a minus takes one out — or the sections: every folder and shelf opens out in place, each with a tick to show or hide it and a handle to drag it where you want it. The page holds still while you edit.
- **Everything as rows.** "Other" is gone: Playlists, Artists, Albums, Podcasts and Subscriptions are rows of the Library like the folders, with Apple Music's icons.
- **A statistics banner, if you want it.** The Replay banner at the top of the Library is an option in Settings, off by default.
- **Covers that travel.** Opening a playlist, album or artist, its cover grows from its card to the head of its page, and goes back to its card when you leave.
- **Pushes.** The Library's pages — a whole shelf, Downloads, History — open with the same push as Settings.
- **Smaller rows.** The Library's text rows are a touch smaller and closer.
- **Likes without an account.** Favorite and like songs without signing into any account, stored locally on your device — with a choice, once signed in, of keeping them on the account or on the device only.
- **"Favorites" playlist.** An automatic favorites collection with its own cover, in My playlists.
- **Nothing leaves on its own.** Hearts and the playlists you create in Auden stay on your device: a heart is no longer sent to YouTube Music, a new playlist is no longer made there, and your plays are no longer registered in your YouTube history. The "Where hearts go" setting is gone, and so is the thumb-down, which only steered your account's recommendations.
- **A library of your own.** The Library no longer reads your YouTube Music or Spotify account live: it shows what is in Auden — your playlists, likes, downloads and what you have brought over. If you were signed in, a note tells you once where your library went.
- **Artists, Albums and Podcasts, from what is yours.** The Library keeps its rows for Playlists, Artists, Albums and Podcasts, and each holds only what is on your device or what you brought over with Transfer your music — never what an account holds live. Transfer your music also brings your followed podcasts.
- **Artists, in their own picture.** In the Library, artists are drawn as their own photo in a circle, and what you play from an artist is remembered in Recents with that photo and not the cover of the song that was playing.
- **A History of your own.** History lists what you have played on this device, most recent first, whether or not you are signed in anywhere.
- **"Playlist" everywhere.** Spanish says "playlist" instead of "lista de reproducción".
- **Folders with covers of their own.** Downloads, Local music, Cached songs and your device lists are special playlists, each with a cover drawn in Apple Music's manner — a gradient and a filled symbol — the same in Recents, on its card and on its own page. Opening one from its row pushes it in like any page, even when it is also in Recents.
- **Liked Music and Favorites in Playlists.** Liked Music leads the Playlists section once YouTube Music is connected, and Favorites has the same kind of cover as the folders.
- **Recents by where it was played.** What you play is remembered by where it was played from — a playlist, an album, a folder, an artist — each with its own picture.
- **Replay, in Apple Music's shape.** The statistics page is redone: Week, Month, Year and All time, each browsable back through its periods, with a slow glow tinted by your top artist, a glass period menu, colourful artist cards, milestones and a closing playlist card.
- **Listening on speakers counts.** What you play on Cast or your watch now adds to your minutes and plays in the listening statistics.

## Import & Transfer

- **Your YouTube Music likes, brought in once.** If you were signed in, the songs you had liked on YouTube Music are copied into your Auden likes the first time you open this version, so no heart is lost. It only reads: nothing is sent back.
- **Where a playlist came from.** Playlists you import remember their origin — YouTube Music or Spotify — and a snapshot of what it held, ready to be updated from it by hand.
- **Transfer your music.** A new button in the account sheet opens one place for every other service: connect YouTube Music or Spotify, pick your likes, playlists, albums and artists, and Auden copies them. It only reads from the service and keeps its own copy; nothing is sent back.
- **A transfer you can watch.** While it runs, a ring fills with the real share done across everything you ticked, shows what is being brought over and whether it is being read, matched or saved, and the bar at the foot moves with the same number.
- **Update a playlist when you want.** A playlist brought over from YouTube Music or Spotify has "Update from its source" in its menu. It shows what changed there (+ added / − removed) and combines it with your copy — what you added stays, what you removed stays removed — or replaces your copy with the source. Nothing updates on its own any more.
- **Export to YouTube Music, by choice.** Sending one of your playlists to YouTube Music is "Export to YouTube Music" in its menu, and asks first: it tells you what will be created or changed there, naming what would be removed.
- **Apple Music and Tidal.** Sign in on their own page, in a sheet over the app, and Auden reads your likes, playlists, albums and artists like any other service and copies them. The session lasts while the app runs, and copies from them are not updated afterwards.
- **Deezer links.** Paste a Deezer playlist or album link beside Spotify's and Auden matches its songs on YouTube Music.
- **Pick by cover.** What a service holds is shown as a grid of covers with a check each, and the Transfer button stays pinned under it. The same steps run in the first-run setup.

## Auden DJ

- **Your own personal DJ.** The DJ is now a section right under Top Picks, with its ring at the top of the banner and what it would say coming in at the foot, word by word, one line at a time — silently, until you press the big play button. What you played lately is a row of song columns, like Trending.
- **Moods and shelves.** Browse and discover DJ sets through dedicated mood categories and shelves directly on the DJ screen, with many more options in each, "Made for you" included.
- **Save sessions as playlists.** Turn any DJ session into a playlist in your library to revisit and replay whenever you want.
- **Natural pronunciation.** A built-in pronunciation engine ensures artist names, song titles and foreign words are spoken naturally by the DJ.
- **Smarter request parser.** A sturdier parser that understands varied, natural phrasing — one artist, several of them, or a name on its own — and searches when it does not know what to play rather than always falling back on the same song.
- **It understands what you ask.** A language model reads your request into a plan — artists, songs by name, what to leave out, decades, genres, mood — and the DJ holds every name against your library, searches YouTube Music for what you do not have and does not let go of what you named. It never picks the songs; without the service, or offline, the DJ reads the request as before. It also names the session in a few words, so what it says does not repeat what you typed.
- **A DJ for new listeners.** With little or nothing in your library or listening, the DJ plays YouTube Music's own charts and genre searches — in Spanish when the DJ speaks Spanish — and what you play from then on becomes the taste its sets follow.
- **It survives a closed app.** The DJ stays on until you stop it: after the app is closed it takes up again on the song that was playing, without starting to play or showing a loading screen, and plans on from there. Its voice lines are no longer kept in the restart queue, so a restart is no longer an ordinary queue with the DJ's recordings between the songs.

## Account

- **An account sheet.** The profile button opens a sheet in the manner of Apple Music's: your account and the profile in use, playback statistics, Listen together, the version you are on and Auden's settings. Drag it down to put it away.
- **Screens stack on it.** What you open from the sheet — Settings, Account and integrations, statistics, Listen together, what's new — slides in from the right over it, as a screen does in any app, and leaves the same way, back to the sheet.
- **Your Auden profile.** Auden has its own profile now — a name and, if you want, a photo kept on your device — and it is you, whatever services you sign into. Edit it in Account settings; Listen Together and Connect use it. Devices that were already signed in keep the same party identity and Connect room, so nothing disconnects.
- **A fuller backup.** The backup now carries your on-device likes and playlists, your name and your profile, so a new device that restores it is you straight away.
- **Nothing asks your account for what to play.** Search, radio, artist cards, lyrics and the DJ's and AutoPlay's suggestions are asked of YouTube Music as nobody — no sign-in behind them — and your searches are no longer written into your YouTube history. The DJ draws on your library, your likes and what you have listened to, and fills in with YouTube Music's own recommendations when that is not enough yet.
- **Your profile is the account.** Your Auden profile's photo — or your initial — is the button on the top bars, and its card heads Account & integrations in place of "Not signed in" or a YouTube Music profile. YouTube Music, Spotify, Apple Music and Tidal sit together as connections in one group.
- **A first-run setup.** A new listener starts straight on a setup drawn like the update's welcome: bring your music — Spotify, YouTube Music, Apple Music, Tidal, a backup, a playlist file or this phone — or start fresh, then your name and photo, a greeting, Automix and reduced blur, and in. The update's welcome is shown only to those who have been through the setup.
- **Google Drive backup.** Settings → Your data → Google Drive backup keeps your listening and settings in a hidden app folder in your Drive, with a schedule (daily, weekly or monthly) and Sync now. Each phone is its own device: the listening of all your devices adds up in Replay, and you can restore this phone as another. Sign-ins are never included.
- **YouTube Premium audio.** With YouTube Music connected, a switch under it in Account & integrations lets Premium accounts stream the higher-quality ~256 kbps Opus.

## Settings

- **Grouped settings.** In the light theme, Settings pages and the screens opened from them stand on the same grey, with white groups, as the sheet does. Every one of them opens and closes with the same push.
- **Cleaner Settings.** Account, Listen together, statistics and what's new moved to the sheet.
- **Regrouped, in Mochy Music's manner.** A card at the top suggests settings that fit what you have been doing; Storage shows one bar for the phone and one for the app with a legend; Manage downloads lists every download with its size, searchable, the heaviest first, and removes the ones you pick; and the foot of Settings carries the version and whose servers the app stands on.
- **System theme by default.** The app follows the phone's light or dark theme until you choose one.
- **Translated into seventeen languages.** The app, the TV and Wear OS apps, the website and the browser extension are translated into Spanish, French, German, Polish, Portuguese, Indonesian, Hindi, Japanese, Russian, Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Hebrew, Italian, Turkish, Thai, Vietnamese and Korean.

## Look & Glass

- **A new name on the mark.** A new Auden logo everywhere — the app, the TV and Wear OS apps, the website and the browser extension — and thirteen launcher icons to pick from: Liquid Glass in light and dark, black, white and nine colours.
- **A launch animation.** The logo's liquid-glass animation replaces the static splash, in light and dark.
- **A welcome to the new look.** Shown once, the first time you open the app after updating: four pages in black with the logo's loop of drops behind them.
- **Your colour, everywhere.** Pick an accent in Settings and it colours the open tab, switches, banners, the main action of every alert and the stats mesh.
- **A reworked nav bar.** The film is clearer through the middle line and thicker along the top and bottom edges, with a thin rim, more saturated colour and a gentler lens. The drop you drag across the bar is smaller and darker-edged, every tab inside it takes the accent colour, and the open tab turns plain white while the drop is out.
- **A bar that follows your scroll.** Scrolling down shrinks the whole expanded bar a little, about its foot, before it folds, and scrolling back up gives the size back. It folds from the size it had reached, at any speed, and only reaching the top of the page unfolds it again.
- **Glow on press.** Pressing a tab lights a soft glow on it while the drop travels there, or for as long as you hold it.
- **A finer rim.** Liquid glass has a thin black line at its ends where the light does not reach, on the bar, the mini player, the menus and the drop.
- **Glass menus.** Menus throughout the app use the liquid glass material, a little denser for legibility and with no lens bending their edges.
- **Restructured song menu.** The ⋯ song menu is reorganised into clear categories with smooth back navigation — and the system's back button now steps back through its pages. Artist, album and song information have a group of their own, and the sleep timer, Automix summary, lyrics offset and log sit under "Other functions".
- **Opacity slider.** Settings has a Glass opacity slider from 0 to 100% (75% by default): the more see-through the glass, the more it blurs and the more muted its colours.
- **Glass or Solid.** One selector in Appearance replaces the Liquid Glass switch, Progressive Blur and "Reduce dynamic blur": Glass is the default, and Solid fills every surface instead of sampling it. The glass performance mode is gone.
- **Library icon.** The Auden mark is cut out of the Library tab's icon, as in Apple Music.
- **Smoother player close.** Letting go of the player after dragging it down no longer darkens the page for a frame, and anything opened from the player closes with it.
- **iOS 26 alerts.** Every alert and dialog is redrawn as iOS 26's: rounder, in the app's glass, with each answer as its own capsule — the main one filled in your accent, the others on a soft grey — and no hairlines between them.
- **One look for all of them.** Dialogs that used the system's own style, and the pop-up cards, follow the same design, with hairlines that start and end inside their cards.
- **The profile alert, rebuilt.** Switching account or profile is a new alert in the new style, in place of the old floating card.
- **Playlist creation, as rows.** Making or importing a playlist lists its options as plain rows of one card instead of blocks set into it.
- **Song information and Automix, rebuilt.** Both are new alerts that open over the player, with the Automix view in the player's own glass.
- **Glass answers a finger.** Glass buttons — the top bar's, the account sheet's close, Search's folded circle and the player bar — swell and light up while you press them, and stay so through a long press.
- **Voice search.** Every search field has a microphone beside the magnifier that opens the phone's own dictation.
- **A back gesture that drags.** The system back gesture drags the page away and shows the one under it; let go before the edge and it comes back.
- **A welcome in the light theme.** The welcome, the update page and the sign-in card follow the theme, with light versions of the logo's clips.

## Connect

- **No Google account needed.** Devices find each other through your Spotify account or the same network.
- **What you start stays where it plays.** A song, album or playlist started from your phone or the web keeps playing on the device that was already playing, instead of taking the session over.
- **The session follows the player.** A mirrored session pauses when the device that was playing drops off the room.

## Updates

- **An update page.** An update opens a page with the logo's loop, the version and the whole changelog, with the download button and its progress always in view. The download carries on in the background when you close the page. The same page shows "About this version" from the account sheet.
- **A fuller icon.** The update icon on Home is a solid disc with a bold arrow.
- **Changelogs by module.** Each release's notes are grouped by the app's modules — Player, Library, Settings… — each one a dropdown that opens only when you press it, here and in Settings.

## Fixes

- Playlists no longer crashes when scrolled down: a list that appeared twice was the cause.
- Direct downloads resume after a reset, deleted files and the releases they emptied are forgotten, and a missing local file no longer loops playback.
- The DJ banner's lines keep their place while you scroll instead of starting over.
- A transfer you ask for brings back the likes you had taken off here.
- The Liquid Glass launcher icons keep their corners inside Samsung's mask.
- A song you add to a playlist is no longer swapped for a file on your device with a similar name: a live, acoustic, remixed or sped-up version, or an artist whose name is only part of another's, is no longer taken for the same song.
